# What was refused: Stage 14

What was weighed in `stage14.md` and refused, each with the condition that would bring it back.

## What "improved" covers

Of `stage14.md`, *What "improved" covers*.

Refused: the three kinds `plan.md` names, and no more. What is not built and said as built, a
stage named in the design, prior art describing nothing and a name stated nowhere would each
stay known and unlisted. No condition brings it back.

Refused: only what a script finds. A claim that is false against the tree is the one defect no
script finds, and the worst a document has. No condition brings it back.

Refused: a sentence that could read better, as a kind. It has no rule to be held to, and a
stage that reads 4,428 lines for it rewrites them. The condition that brings it back is a
reader from outside who says where they stopped following.

## What is said of what is not built

Of `stage14.md`, *What is said of what is not built*.

Refused: left as they are. Four forms, and one section with no mark that reads as built. No
condition brings it back.

Refused: every design that is not built moved to its stage's plan, and back when it is built.
It is decided, and a decision belongs in its document; moved, it is in two places in turn, and
the plan holds design. No condition brings it back.

Refused: the one mark, with no script. A later mention with no mark is found by reading or not
at all. The condition that brings it back is nothing left that is designed and not built.

## A stage in a document of the design

Of `stage14.md`, *A stage in a document of the design*.

Refused: left as they were. A number nothing checks, history in a document of what is, and
they moved once already. No condition brings it back.

Refused, though it was recommended: a stage named by its use. A thing not built citing its
stage by the heading of `plan.md`, history removed, a measurement citing the document that
holds it, and the scan failing on a bare number. Enkrid ruled for no stage at all: a citation

Refused: the same, with history kept and its number taken out. "Before" with nothing to say
before what is a sentence only somebody who was there can follow. No condition brings it back.

## What was found

Of `stage14.md`, *What was found*.

### c: said twice

Refused: a clause that says what a citation beside it is of. The citation is what points, and a
pointer with no word of what it points at cannot be read. Twenty of the thirty: that a native
module's code runs inside a phase; that a refused call fails the invocation when it returns;
what unload drops and keeps, and what becomes of the entities, twice; what a system and an
observer subscribe to; what is never discarded on a call that means keep running; what is kept
elsewhere and reached from the row; what is refused at `add_system`; that a barrier at which
nothing changed writes nothing, and what one writes for a module; the memory a module can write
and Bridge can read, and that it is fixed by the SDK; that a `declare` that does not fit
is refused; that a barrier is worked an entity at a time; that a module's settings are a
component; what holds a share back at unload; what finds a bad write; that a work limit only
goes down; who owns the loop; what is set aside at the last barrier; that a dependency nobody
wrote down is refused; what a loss needs to be accepted. No condition brings it back.

Refused: a phrase two documents use of two things. "On the path this design exists to keep
free", of a load and of a count; "over the tightest loop a system can have", of two
measurements; and "runs a core wasm module in a sandbox", which is what the wasm backend is
called where it is introduced, twice. No condition brings it back.

Refused: an example in the form of the document it is in. The manifest as text in `module.md`,
and as the C that places it in a section in `native.md`. No condition brings it back.

Refused: `design.md` saying in a line what another document states in full: what each backend
is for, and that a module names the one that runs it. It is the document that says all of the
design in short, and cites where each part is. The condition that brings it back is `design.md`
and a document it summarises found to disagree.

Refused: a row of a table. That a module cannot reach memory it was not given is a cell of the
table of `module.md` and a sentence of `capabilities.md`, which owns it; a cell cannot cite. And
the one kind of file the library opens, and what the native backend checks of an export, are
each said in two documents of one seam that both cite the third that owns it. No condition
brings it back.
